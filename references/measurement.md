# Measurement and confidence

Define source, event, count method, attribution window/model, timezone, date basis, export time, filters, deduplication and coverage. Do not assume systems must agree or must disagree. Aligned advertising conversions can be consistent; analytics key events, platform attribution and CRM contacts can represent different measures.

Separate customer conversion delay from processing freshness. Mark provisional results when supported, using actual lag/indicators rather than a universal waiting period. Revisit material conclusions when matured data arrives.

Record dated tracking fixes, event/consent/attribution changes, cross-domain limitations and changed reporting filters. More recorded outcomes after a fix can reflect better measurement. Compare stable business evidence before claiming growth.

Keep source-specific counts and hypotheses separate. Do not reconcile counts without validating identity and counting rules. Explain preferred-source coverage. Different attribution views can overlap and cannot automatically be added as independent contacts.

Classify claims individually: verified observation; supported interpretation; hypothesis requiring a named check; unknown because evidence is missing. A verified change does not verify its cause.

Before/after movement can be confounded by budget, seasonality, audience and simultaneous changes. Reserve causal lift and incrementality for suitable experiments or explicitly justified causal methods. Preserve supplied test confidence settings; never invent significance from a small ranking table.

If a discrepancy could reverse the recommendation, make its investigation a dependency. Continue valid descriptive work and put material qualifications beside the affected claim.
